How to read every chart here

Bars are drawn on one shared scale — percentage of all 866 students — so a bar in section C is directly comparable with one in section A. The faint vertical lines mark 25%, 50% and 75%. Each bar is labelled with the student count first and the percentage second.

Where a question is a scale (A4, A6, B6, C5, C6, C8) the options stay in the form's own order rather than being sorted by size, because sorting a scale makes it unreadable. Options that sit outside the scale — Other, Not sure, It varies — are drawn in grey and pushed to the end. On multi-select questions students could tick several answers, so those bars deliberately do not add to 100%.

A2availability grid · required

Tick every block in which you are reliably free most weeks. Not "could free up if needed", but actually free.

Be honest here. Ticking extra boxes does not help you. It puts you in a team that schedules meetings you cannot attend.

Read with care. 226 students ticked exactly 7 blocks, and 209 of those ticked one block on each of the seven days - a shape that would occur by chance in about 1% of answers. C1single choice · required

A submission is due in three days. One teammate has not sent their part and is not replying. What are you most likely to actually do?

What this chart shows

Each bar is one response to a teammate who has gone silent three days before a deadline. Length is the share of students choosing it; each student picked one, so the bars total 100%.

Call them directly and ask what is going on30134.8%
Quietly start redoing their part myself so the deadline is safe25629.6%
Message the group and ask if someone can check on them19422.4%
Raise it with the mentor809.2%

The most common answer is the direct one - call them. But 30% would quietly redo the missing work themselves, which protects the deadline and hides the problem from everyone who could fix it. That is the answer most likely to produce a team that looks fine until it fails.

C3single choice · required

What this chart shows

The mirror image of C1: what the student would do when they are the one who has fallen behind. Length is the share choosing each response.

Tell the group as soon as I know I will be late54362.7%

Tell one person I am close to and ask them to cover for me12214.1%

63% would tell the group early, which is the answer that keeps a team functional. But one in five would say nothing and try to catch up unnoticed, and a further 14% would ask one person to cover. A third of the cohort, then, would hide being late rather than flag it.
